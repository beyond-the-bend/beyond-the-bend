# Section 1: Accident and Sickness Insurance Overview

### Financial Protection When Alive
This insurance is designed to prevent the loss of income and savings while the client is still alive. The core products include:
*   Disability Insurance
*   Critical Illness Insurance
*   Long Term Care
*   Travel Insurance
*   Standard medical and dental plans

### Longevity Risk
*   Longevity refers to the risk of a person living longer than their projected life expectancy.
*   **Key Highlight:** People who have longevity in their family history should be offered **Long Term Care** insurance.

***

# Section 2: Disability Insurance Details

### Core Principles
*   **Occupation** is the primary factor used to determine the type of coverage, benefit amount, and overall needs.
*   **Individual Disability Insurance:** Can provide up to 60 percent of a client's income if they are unable to work due to accident or sickness, typically covering them up to age 65.
*   **Pre Disability Income:** When assessing needs, the insurer considers all sources of income that will continue after the disability occurs, including trusts, rental income, investments, and interest.
*   **Coverage Window:** Both individual and group policies cover injuries 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and are not limited to work related accidents.

### Exclusions (What is Not Covered)
*   Self inflicted wounds
*   Suicide attempts
*   Normal pregnancy (complications or abnormal pregnancies are covered)
*   Pre existing conditions (injuries or illnesses present before purchasing the policy)
*   Acts of war or terrorist attacks

### Claim Denial Bases
Benefits can be denied due to:
*   **Fraud or Misrepresentation**
*   **Absence of Loss:** The client is not actually disabled and is still able to work.
*   **Absence of Proof:** Lack of medical reports to prove the disability.
*   **Delay:** Failing to notify the insurer within 30 days of the event, or failing to submit proof of disability within 90 days.

### Policy Renewal Types
*   **Non Cancelable Policy:** The insured can cancel, but the insurer cannot. The insurer cannot increase the premium. This is the most expensive type of policy.
*   **Guaranteed Renewal Policy:** Renews automatically every year until age 65. The insurer can increase premiums if claims in the pool rise, but they cannot cancel the policy.
*   **Cancelable Policy:** The cheapest type of policy. The insurer can cancel the policy or raise premiums if they see increased risk.
*   **Non Traditional Plans:** Specific plans designed for self employed or contract workers.

### Key Definitions of Disability
*   **Own Occupation:** The easiest to qualify for, and the most expensive. You are considered totally disabled if you cannot perform the primary duties of your specific regular job, even if you choose to work in a different job.
*   **Regular Occupation:** Similar to own occupation, but if you return to work in *any* other capacity, your benefits are reduced or stopped.
*   **Any Occupation:** The hardest to qualify for, and the cheapest. You are only considered disabled if you cannot perform any job for which you are suited by education, training, or experience.
*   **Recurring Disability:** If the same disability returns after the client went back to work, the waiting period is waived, meaning they do not have to wait a second time for benefits.
*   **Presumptive Disability:** Benefits are paid automatically even if the client can still work. Examples include total loss of hearing, eyesight, or limbs.

### Occupational Risk Classes
Premium rates are determined by occupational risk. Class 4A has the lowest premium, and Class B has the highest:
*   **4A:** Doctors, Lawyers (Lowest risk, lowest premium)
*   **3A:** Office Clerks
*   **2A:** Salespeople
*   **A:** Skilled Manual Workers
*   **B:** Labor and Construction Workers (Highest risk, highest premium)

# Section 3: Group Disability and Asset Protection

### Group Disability
*   **Short Term Disability (STD):** Typically pays 70 to 75 percent of income for up to 12 months. Usually has a 7 day waiting period for accidents and a 15 day waiting period for sickness.
*   **Long Term Disability (LTD):** Typically pays 50 to 60 percent of income. It begins after STD benefits expire and can pay for 2 years, 5 years, 10 years, or until age 65.
*   **Creditor Disability Insurance:** A tax free benefit purchased to cover specific loan payments if the client becomes disabled.

### Asset Protection (Critical Illness and Long Term Care)
*   **Critical Illness (CI):** Pays out a tax free lump sum if diagnosed with a covered condition.
    *   **The Big 4:** Most basic policies cover Heart Attack, Stroke, Cancer, and Coronary Bypass Surgery.
    *   **Survival Clause:** The client must survive a waiting period (usually 30 days) after diagnosis to receive the benefit.
*   **Long Term Care (LTC):** Triggered when a client can no longer perform at least two of the Activities of Daily Living (ADLs):
    1.  Dressing
    2.  Bathing
    3.  Toileting
    4.  Transferring
    5.  Eating
    6.  Maintaining Continence

### Key Riders
*   **ROP (Return of Premium):** Refunds all paid premiums to the client at age 65 if no claims were made.
*   **COLA (Cost of Living Adjustments):** Increases benefit payments to match inflation.
*   **Waiver of Premium:** Stops premium payments while the insured is disabled, resuming them if the insured recovers.

# Expanded Study Summary

### 1. Insurance to Protect Your Business
When a business partner becomes disabled, retired, or passes away, business continuity is protected using a **Buy and Sell Agreement**. These agreements are funded using **Disability Buy Out Insurance**.
*   **Cross Purchase Agreement (also called criss cross):** 
    *   Partners buy policies on each other. Each partner is the owner, payor, and beneficiary of the policy on the other partner.
    *   If one partner becomes disabled, the other partner receives the tax free benefit and uses it to buy out the disabled partner's shares.
    *   The most common definition of disability used in these agreements is **regular occupation**.
*   **Entity Purchase (also called Share Redemption):**
    *   The company itself owns, pays for, and is the beneficiary of the policies on each partner.
    *   If a partner becomes disabled, the company receives the insurance proceeds to buy out and cancel that partner's interest.
*   **Business Overhead Expense (BOE):** Covers actual business expenses (such as rent and utilities) when an owner is disabled, ensuring the office stays open.

### 2. Income Types and Disability Insurance Eligibility
Not all income is treated equally when determining disability benefits.
*   **Earned (Active) Income:** Includes salary, bonuses, commissions, wages, tips, and self employed income.
    *   *Eligible for disability benefits:* **Yes**
    *   *Used in benefit calculations:* **Yes**
*   **Unearned (Passive) Income:** Includes investment income, rental income, child or spousal support, royalties, and trust income.
    *   *Eligible for disability benefits:* **No** (because this income continues even if you are disabled).
    *   *Used in benefit calculations:* **Yes** (the insurer reduces your allowable disability benefit because you still have this passive income coming in).
*   **In Kind Income:** Includes employer benefits (such as a company car, housing, and meals) or free childcare from a relative.
    *   *Eligible for disability benefits:* **No**
    *   *Used in benefit calculations:* **No**

### 3. Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA) Calculations
*   This rider increases benefits annually to keep pace with inflation, often capped at a maximum rate (such as 5 percent). 
*   **Formula:** `New Benefit Amount = Current Amount x (1 + COLA %)`
*   The increases compound annually. For example, if a $3,000 benefit is indexed to a 3.1 percent CPI increase, the new benefit for the next year is `$3,000 x 1.031 = $3,093`. The following year's increase is calculated using the new $3,093 baseline.

### 4. Group Disability and Employment Insurance (EI) Coordination
*   **Short Term Disability (STD):** Typically covers 70 to 75 percent of income for up to 12 months. Benefits begin on the first day for accidents, but usually have a 7 day waiting period for illnesses.
*   **EI Coordination:** 
    *   Government Employment Insurance (EI) provides up to 26 weeks of benefits after a 1 week waiting period.
    *   Because **EI is a second payor**, it will reduce its benefits dollar for dollar if group disability benefits are paid at the same time.
    *   To avoid this offset, group plans stagger payments: they pay a first day benefit for the first week, pause payments for 26 weeks (while the employee receives EI), and resume the group benefits in week 28.

### 5. Taxation of Disability Benefits
Whether your benefits are taxable depends entirely on who paid the premiums:
*   **Individual Policies:** Premiums are not tax deductible, and benefits are **tax free**.
*   **Employer Paid Group Policies:** Premiums are paid by the employer (and deducted as a business expense). The benefits received by the employee are **taxable**.
*   **Employee Paid Group Policies:** Premiums are paid 100 percent by the employee. The benefits received are **tax free**.
*   **Shared Premium Group Policies (Employer and Employee split the cost):** 
    *   The portion of the benefit paid for by the employer is taxable, but **the employee can deduct the total amount of premiums they have personally paid since their last claim** from the taxable benefit amount.
    *   *Example:* If an employee receives $17,000 in total benefits, and has personally paid $4,100 in premiums, only `$17,000 - $4,100 = $12,900` of the benefit is taxable.

# 🧠 High Probability Exam Notes

### 1. Longevity and Long Term Care
*   **The Rule:** If a client has family members who live to a very old age, they should be offered **Long Term Care** insurance.
*   **In Simple Terms:** People with a family history of long lives are highly likely to live a long time themselves. The longer a person lives, the higher the chance they will eventually need help with daily physical tasks (like dressing or bathing) in their old age. Long Term Care insurance pays for this support.

### 2. The Number One Disability Factor
*   **The Rule:** A client's **Occupation** is the primary factor used to determine their disability policy type, benefit amount, and overall need.
*   **In Simple Terms:** Your job is the absolute most important detail when buying disability coverage. It determines your premium cost and what kind of policy you can get. A high risk worker (like a construction worker) will pay much more for coverage than a low risk worker (like a lawyer).

### 3. Gift Money vs. Disability Needs
*   **The Rule:** Cash gifts or regular savings help from a parent cannot be considered when calculating a client's disability insurance needs.
*   **In Simple Terms:** If a client receives monthly money from their mother or family to help them save, the insurance company will ignore it. Gift money is not official earned income, so it cannot be used to calculate how much disability coverage the client is allowed to buy. 

### 4. Continuing Passive Income
*   **The Rule:** The insurer will consider all sources of income that continue after a disability (such as rental, investment, and trust income) before calculating disability needs.
*   **In Simple Terms:** If you get disabled, your passive income (like rent from a tenant or investment dividends) keeps coming in anyway. Because you still have money entering your bank account while disabled, the insurance company will look at this passive income and **reduce** the maximum amount of disability coverage they will sell you.

### 5. Who Owns a Cross Purchase Buy Sell Policy?
*   **The Rule:** In a cross purchase agreement, the partners are the owner, payor, and beneficiary of each other's policy.
*   **In Simple Terms:** If Partner A and Partner B run a business together, they buy policies on each other. Partner A owns, pays for, and is the beneficiary of the policy on Partner B. Partner B does the same for Partner A. If Partner B becomes disabled, Partner A receives the cash benefit and uses it to buy out Partner B's shares.

### 6. The Buy Out Disability Definition
*   **The Rule:** The definition of disability most frequently used in a disability buy out policy is **regular occupation**.
*   **In Simple Terms:** In a business buyout agreement, partners need to agree on exactly when someone is disabled enough to trigger the sale of their shares. They use the *regular occupation* definition, which means the buyout is triggered if the partner cannot perform the main duties of their specific regular job.

### 7. Who Owns an Entity Purchase Policy?
*   **The Rule:** In an entity purchase agreement, the company is the owner, payor, and beneficiary.
*   **In Simple Terms:** Instead of the partners owning policies on each other, the business itself (the company) owns, pays for, and is the beneficiary of the policies on all the partners. If a partner becomes disabled, the company gets the money and uses it to buy back and cancel that partner's interest.

### 8. Compounded COLA Increases
*   **The Rule:** `New Amount = Current Amount x (1 + COLA %)`
*   **In Simple Terms:** A Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA) rider helps your disability benefit grow with inflation. This increase is **compounded** every year. This means next year's increase is calculated using your new, higher monthly benefit, not the original amount you started with.

### 9. Waiting Periods: Accident vs. Illness
*   **The Rule:** Benefits begin on the first day for disabilities caused by accidents, but have a 7 day waiting period for disabilities caused by illnesses.
*   **In Simple Terms:** If you are disabled by an unexpected event like a car crash, your policy pays out from **day one** (no waiting). If you are disabled by a sickness or disease (like pneumonia), you must wait **7 days** before your benefits start accumulating.

## Question / Topic


> **Scenario/Question:**


### Explanation & Answer


The correct answer is the first option: **Workers' Compensation benefits are tax-free**.

Here is a simple and detailed breakdown of why this is the correct answer and the tax status of each of these three government benefits. This is a very common exam topic!

***

### 1. Workers' Compensation (Tax-Free)
*   **Tax Status:** **Tax free**
*   **Why:** Workers' Compensation benefits are paid out to employees who are injured on the job or suffer a work related illness. Under Canadian tax law, these benefits are not subject to income tax. While you must report them on your tax return for calculation purposes, you receive an offsetting deduction that reduces the tax on this money to zero.

A **return of premium** (often abbreviated as ROP) rider is essentially a **money back guarantee** on your insurance policy. 

Normally, if you buy insurance and stay healthy, you never get your premium money back. The insurance company keeps it. 

When you add a return of premium rider, the insurer agrees that **if you do not make any claims for a set period of time, they will refund the premiums you paid back to you**.

### A Simple Example
*   You buy a critical illness policy with a return of premium rider that matures at age 65.
