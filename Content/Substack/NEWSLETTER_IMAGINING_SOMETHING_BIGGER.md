# Imagining Something Bigger

Hello everyone,

This week, I found myself imagining something bigger. It is not something I am ready to fully name yet, but it is a possibility that is asking me to stretch beyond what has felt familiar and manageable.

As I allowed myself to sit with it, I noticed two very different responses rising up in me. One part felt alive and hopeful, genuinely excited by the possibility. Another part started searching immediately for every reason to pull back.

It reminded me how easily we can confuse safety with staying small.

***

## This Week's Wellness Wisdom

![A soft, expansive natural landscape](expansive_nature.png)

I have noticed that for me, safety has often come to mean keeping life contained. I avoid asking for too much, taking up too much space, becoming too visible, or reaching for something that could end in disappointment. I have called this being practical, careful, realistic, or responsible, and sometimes it genuinely is. Other times, I have come to understand, it has been an old protective response quietly directing my choices.

This tends to happen when life has felt unpredictable, when our needs have not always been welcomed, or when being fully seen has led to criticism, conflict, or rejection. Smallness begins to feel safe. If we keep our hopes modest, perhaps they cannot be taken from us. If we do not reach too far, perhaps we cannot fail. If we stay quiet, perhaps we cannot be judged. If we keep everything familiar and controlled, perhaps nothing will catch us off guard.

What I have learned is that this protective smallness likely served us well at some point. It probably helped us move through relationships, environments, or stages of life in which shrinking ourselves felt like the wisest or safest thing we could do. The difficulty is that something which protects us in one season can begin to confine us in another.

Eventually, staying small creates its own kind of discomfort. We may feel restless, heavy, disconnected, or quietly dissatisfied without fully understanding why. I know that feeling well. Somewhere within us, there is a part asking for more room, more honesty, more expression, more possibility, or simply more life.

This does not mean we need to force ourselves into enormous change or leap toward something before we are ready. Expansion does not have to be dramatic. It can be slow, thoughtful, and grounded.

What I am finding is that it begins with allowing ourselves to want something before we know exactly how it will happen. It may look like speaking one honest sentence, taking one small step, trying something unfamiliar, or allowing ourselves to be seen a little more clearly. It may simply mean noticing our fear without automatically letting that fear make the decision for us.

I no longer believe that safety is created only by making life smaller and more predictable. Sometimes safety grows through the experience of staying connected to ourselves while we move into unfamiliar territory. It comes from knowing that we will listen when our body asks us to slow down, that we will not force ourselves to move faster than we are able, and that we will ask for help when we need it.

Perhaps safety is not the absence of uncertainty. Perhaps it is knowing that we will remain beside ourselves as we move through it.

I believe there is an important difference between pushing beyond our limits and gently expanding them, between abandoning ourselves in pursuit of a goal and inviting our whole self to come along. I am learning that grounded expansion allows us to take one step and notice how it feels. It allows us to want something without demanding that it happen immediately. It allows us to grow without rushing, to become more visible without leaving ourselves behind.

This week, I have been sitting with the possibility that I do not have to choose between feeling safe and living fully. What I am coming to believe is that the work is not to eliminate fear before we move forward. It is to build enough inner steadiness that fear no longer gets to make every decision.

***

## Practice of the Week: Becoming Curious

As you move through the coming week, you might gently notice where you have been keeping yourself small. This is not an invitation to criticize yourself or to pressure yourself into change. It is simply an opportunity to become curious.

Is there a desire you have been afraid to acknowledge?

A part of yourself you have kept hidden?

A possibility that feels both exciting and frightening?

You do not have to make a giant leap. You may simply be ready to give that part of yourself a little more space.

***

## A Closing Thought

Safety is not always found by reducing our lives until nothing can surprise or hurt us. I am learning that sometimes it grows when we discover that we can meet desire, visibility, change, and uncertainty without leaving ourselves behind.
