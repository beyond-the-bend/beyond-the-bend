# When the Mind Steeps Too Long

Hello,

I hope your week is starting with a sense of ease. Lately, I have been noticing how quickly my mind can drift away from the simple beauty of the present moment, turning even the quietest afternoon into a series of problems to solve. It is a subtle shift, one that often happens before I even realize I have left my body behind.

This week, a simple cup of tea reminded me of what happens when we stay in those loops just a little too long. I wanted to share some reflections on that internal shift, along with a gentle invitation to return to yourself.

---

## When the Mind Steeps Too Long

There is a point where thinking stops helping and starts clouding everything.

There are moments when I can feel it happening, not as anything dramatic or visible from the outside, but as a quiet internal shift, subtle enough that I might miss it if I am not paying attention.

My mind begins to circle. One thought finds another and then another, and somewhere in that movement I lose the thread of where I actually am. I am no longer here, in my life, in my body. I have drifted up into my head, turning things over, trying to solve something, trying to understand, trying to land somewhere solid. And the longer I stay there, the heavier everything becomes.

I noticed it the other day while making tea. The bag goes in and for a moment it is just right, the water warming and deepening, something alive moving through it. But if you leave it too long the whole thing shifts, growing darker and more bitter until what started out as something nourishing becomes something you no longer want to drink.

The mind can do this too, and I think most of us know exactly what that feels like even if we have never had a word for it.

We need our thoughts. We need that capacity to reflect, to discern, to move through our days with some sense of direction, and that is not the problem. The problem is when we forget to lift the bag out, when we stay in the thinking past the point where it is actually helping us. And the strange thing is that in those moments we almost always believe the answer is more thinking, that if we go a little deeper or look a little harder we will finally arrive somewhere solid. But what is actually needed tends to be much simpler than that.

Just a pause. A gentle return to the body, to the breath, to the feeling of feet on the ground and shoulders releasing without being asked. Nothing in my life has changed in that moment, and yet something in me has. The water clears, and if a thought comes from that quieter place it tends to be different, not tangled or urgent, just clean and simple and enough.

This is what the practice keeps teaching me, not to silence the mind or distrust it, but to recognize when it has done its work, to know the moment when more thinking will not bring more clarity. Stepping back from that kind of looping is not avoidance or giving up. It is, I think, a form of wisdom we are all slowly learning how to trust.

## Practice of the Week: Learning to Pause

The next time you notice your mind looping, try this simple sequence.

**Notice what is actually there.** Before you do anything else, just look at what the mind is holding. Not to judge it or fix it, but to see it clearly. Are there the same thoughts circling back around? Name it, even silently to yourself. The mind is busy. That is enough to know.

**Pause.** Not to fix anything or arrive anywhere, but simply to interrupt the momentum of it. You do not need to change what is happening, you just need to step out of the current for a moment. Even one minute is enough.

**Drop into the body.** Feel your feet on the ground. Notice if your jaw is holding anything, if your shoulders have crept upward, if there is a kind of bracing somewhere that you did not consciously choose. The body almost always knows before the mind does that something needs to soften.

**Breathe.** Take a slow breath in and let the exhale be longer than feels necessary. Let that breath be a gentle signal to the nervous system that it is safe to set the thinking down for a moment.

**Trust.** This is not a practice of emptying or escaping. It is a practice of learning to trust that clarity does not always arrive through effort. Sometimes the wisest thing we can do is stop reaching, and let the water clear.
