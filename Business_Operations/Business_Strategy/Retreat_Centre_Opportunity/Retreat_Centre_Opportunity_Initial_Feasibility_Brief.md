## The Opportunity

There is a possible opportunity to develop a wellness retreat and hospitality business on a smaller, intimate property that retains beautiful land and kind, gentle horses.

The concept could become a hybrid retreat centre, nature based Airbnb, equine wellness space, and workshop venue.

The strongest version of this idea is not only a place to sleep. It is a place where people come to reset, be held by land, experience horses safely and respectfully, attend meaningful retreats, and step out of the pace of ordinary life.

It could also become Laura's main teaching home. This means the property would not only serve occasional retreats or guest stays. It could hold weekly yoga classes, private somatic clients, small group programs, workshops, and the future evolution of Beyond the Bend.

## Why This Has Real Potential

This opportunity has several strong ingredients already in place.

1. The land is emotionally compelling.

The natural setting gives the business room to feel spacious, private, and restorative. That matters for retreats, grief work, nervous system support, women’s circles, equine work, yoga weekends, corporate resets, and creative intensives.

2. The house is sized for intimate groups.

The home could potentially hold workshops, meals, overnight guests, small retreats, private rentals, and facilitator gatherings, depending on layout, bedrooms, bathrooms, parking, fire code, septic capacity, insurance, and zoning.

Early comparison point: Empria's house at Rising Moon can reportedly sleep about 20 people. This house is a smaller, intimate space and is expected to sleep exactly 14 people. That capacity still needs to be confirmed against bedrooms, beds, bathrooms, egress, fire safety, septic, parking, insurance, and zoning, but it grounds the retreat math in reality.

3. The horses create a distinct identity.

Many retreat spaces have land. Fewer have calm, well cared for horses. The horses could become the heart of the brand, but this needs to be done carefully, ethically, and with proper training.

4. The offer can have multiple income streams.

This matters because a retreat property should not depend on one single type of booking. The strongest version would combine personal retreats, Airbnb stays, rented workshop weekends, Laura led retreats, and equine facilitated wellness.

5. The space could reduce or replace Laura's room rental costs.

If Laura teaches weekly classes and private clients on this property, the space becomes part of the core operating model of Beyond the Bend. This could make the business stronger because it creates consistency, atmosphere, brand identity, and a deeper relationship between students and the land.

## Important Legal And Regulatory Notes

Alberta increased the tourism levy from 4 percent to 6 percent effective April 1, 2026. Alberta also requires operators, accommodation hosts, and online brokers who collect payment for temporary accommodation to understand registration, collection, reporting, and remittance obligations.

Equine facilitated work also needs careful scope. Pro EFW Canada describes equine facilitated wellness as an umbrella term for respectful partnership with horses in human services, learning, therapy, and wellness. CanTRA provides education, instructor certification, and centre accreditation related to therapeutic riding and equine facilitated work.

The safest early positioning is wellness, reflection, nervous system support, and mindful horse connection, not medical treatment or psychotherapy.

If Laura completes equine certification, the scope can expand, but the language should still be precise. Unless Laura is working with a licensed mental health professional or has a regulated therapeutic credential that allows it, the public language should avoid promising treatment for trauma, anxiety, depression, or medical conditions. The stronger and safer language is equine facilitated wellness, horse supported reflection, nervous system awareness, somatic support, and personal integration.

## Best First Phase

The best first phase is to test demand with the existing house, maintaining strict boundaries on capacity.

Phase one:

```text
Clarify the owner relationship and possible business structure.

Confirm zoning, insurance, and safety requirements.

Confirm whether Laura can use the property as a weekly teaching home and private client space.

Create a simple weekly class and private client schedule.

Create and test one full day corporate Team Reset offer for ten to twelve people.

Choose an equine wellness training pathway, then confirm insurance, professional scope, staffing, waivers, and horse welfare requirements.

Build an operating cost model using the property's actual utilities, maintenance, mortgage, taxes, cleaning, staffing, food, and insurance costs.

Photograph the land, house, horses, gathering spaces, bedrooms, bathrooms, trails, views, and outdoor areas.

Create a simple concept deck.

Run one invitation only day retreat.

Run one small paid women’s retreat.

Test one private Airbnb style weekend rental.

Ask two outside facilitators if they would rent the space for a workshop.

Track inquiries, revenue, costs, guest experience, and operational friction.
```

If phase one works, then move to better branding, stronger booking systems, and small infrastructure upgrades.

## What Laura Should Bring To The Owner

The conversation should not begin with expensive building ideas. It should begin with a staged vision.

Suggested message:

```text
I think this property has the potential to become a beautiful retreat and wellness destination, and also the home base for my weekly classes and private client work.
```
