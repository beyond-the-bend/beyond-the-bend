### My Story Was Never Too Small

*By Laura Harvey | Beyond the Bend*

My life has been full. Full of joy, full of good ordinary days, moments I would not trade for anything. And woven right through all of that goodness, there have also been things that hurt. Really hurt. More than I could ever count.

For a long time, I did not let myself look at any of it. Somewhere along the way I had decided that my pain was not big enough to deserve my own attention. Other people had been through worse. Who was I to make a fuss over what happened to me. So I did not make a fuss. I just kept living, the way I imagine you have kept living too.

Maybe you know this feeling. Maybe you have your own version of it, a quiet belief that what you went through was not bad enough to count, that someone else's suffering somehow cancels out your right to feel your own.

Here is what I have learned. The pain does not disappear just because we decide it does not qualify. It goes somewhere else instead. For me, it went into my body. It became tension I stopped noticing because it had been there so long. It became parts of me that went quiet, places that felt strangely dead, and I could understand why until I finally started paying attention.

I wish I could tell you there was one single moment when everything changed. A conversation, a session, a morning I woke up different. It would make for a better story. But that is not how it happened, and I have stopped wishing it were.

It came slowly instead. In glimpses. A moment here where I noticed something in my body and did not immediately look away from it. A moment there where I let myself wonder what was underneath a tightness I had ignored for years. Little by little, I started to believe I was allowed to look. Not because the pain had gotten bigger or more important, but because I had started to love myself enough to think I was worth the looking.

That is really what permission turned out to be. Not a dramatic release. Just small acts of staying present with myself instead of turning away, again and again, until eventually staying became easier than leaving.

If you are waiting for one clear moment before you feel allowed to heal, I want to gently tell you that it may never come, and it does not need to. You do not need a single moment. You only need one small glimpse of willingness. Then another.

For me it felt like tiny bits of sunlight finding their way in, small enough that I almost missed them. But I could feel the warmth. And something in me softened, just a little, just enough to make me believe I was worth looking a little deeper for. That belief led to more belief. Small glimpses became something larger, until one day I could see the whole picture clearly, and it felt like sinking into a clear, beautiful stream of water.

I will tell you honestly, the process is not finished. I still find pieces of myself that need tending, pieces that are still learning they are allowed to be seen. I do not know if that ever fully ends, and I have made peace with that. Because every time I look, every time I choose to stay instead of turning away, I am proving something to myself that took me far too long to believe. That I was always worthy of this. That my pain was always worth tending to, simply because it was mine.

And so is yours.

## Practice of the Week

### Two Minutes of Staying

This week, I want to invite you into a practice of staying. It is a simple practice, but it is not easy. It requires no special setup, only a timer and a willingness to be present with whatever is actually here.

To begin, find a quiet place to sit comfortably. Set a timer for exactly two minutes. Close your eyes or let your gaze rest softly on the floor. Take a few natural breaths, letting your shoulders drop and your belly soften. Now, bring your awareness into your body and locate a physical sensation of discomfort. It might be a tightness in your chest, a dull ache in your shoulders, a clenching in your jaw, or even a quiet flutter of anxiety in your stomach. Do not try to change it or make it feel better. Simply place your attention on it, noticing its shape, its temperature, or its texture.

As you sit with this sensation, you will almost certainly feel the urge to look away. You might feel a sudden impulse to stretch, to shift your position, to check your phone, or to start planning the rest of your day. This is a natural protective habit. When that urge arises, notice it without judgment. See if you can take just one more slow breath, staying exactly where you are. Tell that tight or uncomfortable place in your body, silently: *I am here, and I am willing to stay.* Let yourself rest in that willingness, one breath at a time, until the timer rings. When you finish, notice if anything has softened, simply because you chose not to turn away.

There is something beautiful in being imperfect and whole at the same time, in always having more of ourselves to discover. The more we learn, the more we find ourselves to love.
