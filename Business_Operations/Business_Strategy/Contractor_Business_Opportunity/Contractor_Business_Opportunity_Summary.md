# Contractor Business Opportunity

## Purpose

This document summarizes Laura's Claude conversation from June 16 and June 17, 2026 about a possible insurance restoration and general contracting business involving Laura, Shane, and possibly Jordy.

This is a working business summary, not legal or licensing advice. All requirements should be confirmed directly with the relevant Alberta authorities, municipalities, insurers, an insurance broker, an accountant, and a lawyer before money is invested or contracts are accepted.

## The Opportunity

Shane is an experienced HVAC tradesperson with current trade certification, extensive practical experience, industry relationships, and an existing flow of work. His challenge appears to be the administrative side of business, including paperwork, organization, follow up, vendor applications, scheduling, estimates, invoicing, and maintaining formal business requirements.

Laura could provide the organization, communication, project coordination, and business administration that would help Shane move away from many scattered small jobs and toward larger insurance restoration contracts.

The opportunity may eventually grow into a property restoration or insurance restoration company that coordinates multiple trades. Laura would manage clients, adjusters, projects, documentation, finances, and systems. Qualified tradespeople would complete the regulated work.

## Important Clarification

There may not be one universal vendor licence that applies to every insurance company. Each insurer, restoration network, property manager, or third party claims administrator may have its own contractor approval process and preferred vendor list.

The first task is therefore to establish exactly what Shane previously had, what remains current, what expired, who contracted with him, and whether he was an approved vendor himself or worked under another contractor.

## Possible First Phase

The safest first phase is a limited trial conducted through Shane's existing business structure, provided a lawyer and accountant confirm that the arrangement is lawful and properly documented.

In this phase:

1. Shane remains the contractor of record.
2. Contracts and client payments remain connected to Shane's business.
3. Laura provides administrative, operational, and project coordination services.
4. Laura receives clearly defined compensation for her work.
5. Jordy may be paid to develop business systems, a website, communication workflows, document management, and job tracking.
6. Everyone uses a written agreement that remains effective regardless of changes in Laura and Shane's personal relationship.
7. The arrangement is tested before Laura creates a separate restoration company.

## Laura's Possible Role

Laura's work could include:

1. Organizing Shane's existing records and credentials.
2. Identifying missing registrations, coverage, and documents.
3. Contacting insurers and claims administrators.
4. Completing contractor and vendor applications.
5. Communicating with adjusters and clients.
6. Coordinating schedules and subcontractors.
7. Tracking estimates, contracts, change orders, invoices, and payments.
8. Building consistent office procedures.
9. Supporting larger bids and restoration projects.
10. Creating a professional and responsive client experience.

## Shane's Possible Role

Shane could provide:

1. Current trade credentials and technical expertise.
2. Estimating and assessment of the physical work.
3. Existing industry relationships and introductions.
4. Oversight of regulated trade work.
5. Site supervision and quality control.
6. Information about his former vendor relationships.
7. His existing business, insurance, bonding, WCB, and licensing records.
8. A network of qualified tradespeople for larger projects.

## Jordy's Possible Role

Jordy could build the operating systems that make the business efficient and scalable. This might include:

1. A professional website and business email system.
2. Lead intake and client communication.
3. Estimate, job, and invoice tracking.
4. Automated reminders and follow up.
5. Document storage and naming systems.
6. Dashboards for active projects and payments.
7. A reusable operating system that may later be offered to other contractors.

## Questions That Must Be Professionally Verified

1. Who is legally permitted to hold each type of contract?
2. What provincial and municipal registrations apply?
3. Is a prepaid contracting business licence required?
4. What permits are required for each trade and project?
5. Whose insurance responds if there is damage, injury, delay, or defective work?
6. What liability insurance limits are required by insurers and commercial clients?
7. Is bonding required, and whose name must appear on the bond?
8. What WCB responsibilities apply to the contractor of record and every subcontractor?
9. Can Laura perform project coordination through Shane's business, and how should she be classified and paid?
10. What written agreements are needed between Laura, Shane, Jordy, and any subcontractors?
11. What tax, GST, payroll, and bookkeeping requirements apply?
12. What credentials are required to join each insurer or claims administrator network?

## Recommended Sequence

1. Complete the Shane information questionnaire.
2. Gather copies of all current and former business documents.
3. Create a written list of unknowns and missing items.
4. Verify requirements with Alberta authorities and the relevant municipalities.
5. Speak with an Alberta business lawyer and accountant.
6. Speak with a commercial insurance broker about liability and bonding.
7. Choose one limited pilot arrangement.
8. Put compensation, ownership, responsibilities, access to records, and exit terms in writing.
9. Test the working relationship before creating a larger company.
10. Review the results after the first few projects.

## Long Term Possibility

If the trial is successful, Laura could eventually establish a separate property restoration company that contracts directly with clients or insurers and hires Shane and other qualified tradespeople as subcontractors.

That transition should happen only after Laura understands the industry, has reliable systems, has verified the legal and insurance structure, and has enough revenue to support the additional responsibilities.

## Central Decision

The immediate question is not whether Laura should launch a large restoration company. The immediate question is whether Shane has the credentials, records, relationships, business structure, and genuine commitment needed for a small and carefully documented trial.
