## Working Understanding of Grief

Grief does not need to be erased, justified, or completed. Beverly's grief reflects real bonds and real losses. Acceptance does not mean liking what happened, agreeing that it should have happened, or no longer feeling pain. It means gradually reducing the additional struggle against the reality that the losses occurred and learning that grief can be present without occupying every part of every moment.

Letting go should not be framed as letting go of her children, her mother, their importance, or her love. A more appropriate meaning is loosening the need to solve the unsolvable, loosening self judgment, allowing protection to soften only when enough safety is present, and releasing the demand that she must either hold everything together or collapse completely.

Healthy grief support can include movement between attention to the loss and attention to restoration. Restoration may include rest, nourishment, flowers, grandchildren, practical tasks, friendship, movement, and moments in which grief is not the sole focus. Moving away from grief temporarily is not avoidance or betrayal. It can be part of the body's natural rhythm of coping.
