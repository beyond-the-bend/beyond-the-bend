# Equine Wellness Centre Market Research

Created June 17, 2026

## Research Scope

This research examined equine facilitated wellness, equine assisted learning, equine supported counselling, private sessions, group intensives, and horse centred retreats in Alberta and elsewhere in Canada.

The purpose was to understand what comparable businesses offer, what clients currently pay, what training may be required, and how horses could eventually become part of the proposed retreat property.

## Biggest Finding

There is a real and established market for horse supported wellness experiences. The market includes several distinct levels:

```text
Individual equine supported sessions:
Approximately $155 to $270 per session

Six hour group intensives:
Approximately $250 per person

Three day rustic equine retreats:
Approximately $1,250 per person

Three day premium equine retreats:
Approximately $2,400 to $2,900 per person
```

The strongest commercial opportunity for Beyond the Bend would not be ordinary riding lessons or clinical psychotherapy. It would be a carefully positioned combination of somatic support, nervous system awareness, restorative practice, reflective coaching, nature, and respectful interaction with horses.

## Important Language Distinctions

The terms used to describe this work matter.

### Equine Assisted Psychotherapy

This is mental health treatment involving a qualified mental health professional and an appropriately trained equine professional. It may address diagnosed mental health concerns and may qualify for insurance reimbursement when delivered by an eligible regulated practitioner.

Laura should not describe her independent work as psychotherapy, counselling, trauma treatment, or mental health treatment unless she later earns the necessary regulated credential or works in direct partnership with a qualified mental health professional.

### Equine Facilitated Wellness

Pro EFW Canada uses this as an umbrella term for respectful horse and human partnership in wellness, learning, and therapeutic settings. Its certification streams include mental health, learning, and equine facilitated human development.

This language could suit the larger property concept, provided Laura completes appropriate training, obtains insurance, and stays within her professional scope.

### Equine Assisted Learning

This work uses horse interactions to support self awareness, communication, leadership, boundaries, confidence, emotional regulation, and personal development. It is generally experiential and does not require riding.

This may be one of the best descriptions for workshops, group experiences, leadership events, and corporate retreats.

### Horse Supported Somatic Wellness

This is a useful public facing description for Laura's eventual offering. It communicates the experience without claiming to provide regulated therapy.

Possible positioning:

```text
A private land, body, and horse supported experience for women who are carrying too much and need a steady place to reconnect with themselves.
```

## Training And Certification

Equine training does not necessarily have to cost $5,900. There are inexpensive online certificate courses, including the Efficient Therapist Equine Therapy Certification currently advertised at CA$127. The important question is not simply whether a certificate is awarded. The important question is what training, practical experience, professional recognition, and insurance eligibility the certificate actually provides.

## Efficient Therapist Online Certificate

The Efficient Therapist course is a fully online, beginner friendly program advertised as approximately forty hours of study.

Published sale price observed June 17, 2026:

```text
CA$127
```

The provider states that the course is accredited through ICAHP, IPHM, CMA, CPD, and NCCAP. It also describes the certificate as insurance eligible.

The course includes ten modules covering horse behaviour, somatic awareness, nervous system literacy, session design, ethics, risk management, nature based practices, coaching tools, and practice development.

The provider also states:

```text
No previous experience is required

The program is fully online

It may be completed in approximately forty hours

Module quizzes are optional

Certification is awarded after passing an online multiple choice examination with a score of at least eighty percent
```

The course does not advertise required supervised sessions with clients, required practical assessment with horses, demonstrated horsemanship competency, mentorship, first aid, or an onsite skills examination.

Strategic takeaway:

This appears to be an affordable way to learn vocabulary, concepts, session ideas, and basic frameworks. It could be useful as an introductory course or an early research step.

It should not automatically be treated as equivalent to supervised professional certification. Before relying on it to offer paid sessions in Alberta, Laura should obtain written answers from her prospective insurer confirming that this exact certificate is accepted for professional liability and commercial horse related coverage. She should also confirm whether the property insurer requires hands on equine training, documented horse experience, first aid, a second trained facilitator, or a recognized Canadian professional designation.

The word accredited can mean that a private course has been reviewed by a membership or continuing education organization. It does not necessarily mean that the credential is regulated by the Canadian government, recognized by a Canadian clinical profession, or sufficient on its own for safe independent work with people and horses.

## Alberta Legal And Insurance Findings

Research completed June 17, 2026 did not identify an Alberta law requiring a person to hold Pro EFW certification, or any particular equine wellness certificate, in order to offer nonclinical equine assisted learning, mindful horse connection, coaching, or horse supported wellness.

This means the $5,900 Pro EFW pathway does not appear to be a legal requirement for the type of nonclinical experience Laura is considering.

Alberta does regulate particular health professions, protected professional titles, restricted health activities, and some forms of psychosocial treatment. The Government of Alberta states that psychosocial interventions may become restricted activities when they are performed with the intention of treating specific disorders.

The safest distinction is:

```text
Laura may offer:

Equine assisted learning

Horse supported wellness

Mindful horse connection

Somatic awareness

Personal reflection

Stress reduction practices

Coaching and personal development

Laura should not independently claim to provide:

Psychotherapy

Psychological treatment

Diagnosis

Treatment of trauma disorders

Treatment of anxiety or depression

Clinical counselling
```

If a regulated mental health professional becomes part of the program, that professional can provide clinical services within their own scope and insurance.

## What CMA Accreditation Means

The CMA referenced by Efficient Therapist is the Complementary Medical Association. It is not the Canadian Medical Association.

The Efficient Therapist certificate is also advertised through ICAHP, the International Compliance Assurance for Holistic Practitioners. ICAHP offers members access to discounted insurance through Westminster Insurance in the United Kingdom.

ICAHP itself states that recognition may vary by country and that students should review local regulations to determine whether a certificate aligns with regional requirements.

Therefore, CMA accreditation and ICAHP insurance eligibility do not automatically prove that a Canadian equine insurer will cover this particular activity in Alberta.

There is also a reason to verify the provider's marketing carefully. In September 2025, Traditional Chinese Medicine Ontario published a letter raising concerns about a different forty hour Efficient Therapist certificate. The letter questioned whether the provider's accreditation language could cause students to misunderstand the professional standing of that certificate. This does not prove that the equine course is invalid, but it reinforces the need to rely on written confirmation from an Alberta insurer rather than the sales page alone.

## What Canadian Equine Insurers Emphasize

Canadian equine insurance guidance focuses on the actual operation, the experience of the people handling the horses, the services being provided, written procedures, waivers, emergency planning, and appropriate liability coverage.

Henry Equestrian states that an equine assisted services business requires commercial coverage and may also need equine professional liability. Its guidance recognizes that equine assisted businesses may involve several different roles and credential combinations.

Acera Insurance offers equine coach and trainer coverage to people with at least five years of riding experience. Its published eligibility information emphasizes experience rather than one mandatory certification.

Neither source publishes a rule stating that Pro EFW certification is mandatory.

This supports the idea that Shane's lifetime of horse experience could be highly relevant. A sensible division of responsibility could be:

```text
Laura:

Somatic practices

Restorative movement

Nervous system education

Reflection and personal development

Participant care and facilitation

Shane:

Horse selection

Horse handling

Horse behaviour and welfare

Participant safety around horses

Facility and emergency procedures
```

This partnership may be more credible to an insurer than Laura taking an online certificate and then working alone with unfamiliar horses.

## Direct Conclusion

The current evidence does not support spending $5,900 simply because Pro EFW says that its pathway is the professional route.

The CA$127 course could be sufficient as Laura's educational certificate for a carefully described nonclinical horse supported wellness or learning experience if all of the following are true:

```text
An Alberta or Canadian insurer confirms the exact activities in writing

The commercial property policy permits paying participants around the horses

Laura does not market herself as a psychologist, psychotherapist, or clinical counsellor

The program does not claim to diagnose or treat mental health disorders

Shane, or another genuinely experienced horse professional, is physically present and responsible for horse safety

The business uses appropriate waivers, screening, emergency procedures, and incident documentation

Laura and Shane have current first aid training

The first programs are ground based and do not include riding
```

If a Canadian insurer accepts this arrangement, there is no clear reason from the research that Laura must also purchase the $5,900 certification.

If the insurer refuses coverage without a particular recognized practical credential, then the decision becomes an insurance requirement rather than a general legal requirement. Laura could then compare Pro EFW with other practical Canadian programs instead of assuming the most expensive option is necessary.
