When Money Is Really About Safety

By Laura Harvey | Beyond the Bend Yoga

What if our relationship with money isn't really about money at all? What if it's about safety?

I keep coming back to this idea that our bodies don't really speak in numbers. They speak in feeling. And that language gets set down so early, long before we even understand what a bank account is. Think about it. As kids, we're not calculating risk in our heads. We're just sensing it. We can feel whether the adults around us seem steady or unpredictable, whether our needs are actually going to be met, whether the ground under us can be trusted. That felt sense, whatever it was for each of us, seems to become a kind of blueprint we carry forward without even realizing it.

So if things felt tense or unpredictable or scarce when we were little, I don't think our nervous system understood that as a money problem. I think it just understood it as unsafe, full stop, because that was the only category it had. It's only later, once we're adults living in a world that runs on money, that this old wiring finds somewhere new to show up. Money becomes one of the clearest places where that old feeling gets triggered again. Not because money is really the problem, but maybe because it's just the loudest, most obvious place for our body to say what it's been carrying all along. Which is probably why we can be capable and responsible and still feel our stomach drop looking at a bank statement. It was never really about the numbers. It was that same old question underneath. Am I safe?

I've also been reading Eastern Body, Western Mind, which looks at the chakra system through a psychological lens, and something in it stopped me in my tracks. The root chakra is all about foundation. Safety, stability, belonging, that sense of having solid ground under you. And it's said to form in those same early years, shaped by whether things around us felt reliable and whether the people raising us felt steady. When that foundation feels solid, we tend to move through life trusting that we'll be okay. When it doesn't, we can carry this quiet background hum that the ground isn't quite trustworthy, even when everything in front of us is actually fine. I don't think it's a coincidence that money gets linked to this chakra so often. I don't think money is ever really the story. I just think it's one of the places where the real story finally becomes visible.

Whether that language makes more sense to you through the nervous system, through the chakras, or just through your own experience, I keep landing on the same question. Lately I'm less interested in asking how I can make more money, and more curious about something quieter. What actually helps me feel safe? Maybe that's where things start to shift. Not by forcing ourselves to think differently, and not by pretending the fear isn't there, but by just noticing how our body responds and offering ourselves a little of the same kindness we hand out to everyone else without even thinking about it.

Practice of the Week

Before You Look at the Numbers

The next time you check your bank account, pay a bill, or think about money, pause for a moment before looking at the numbers.

Notice your body. What happens in your breathing? What happens in your chest or your belly? What thoughts arrive first?

Rather than trying to change anything, simply become curious. Awareness is often the first step toward healing.

A Closing Thought

I tried this practice myself a few days after that first unsettling moment at my bank account. I paused before I looked. I noticed the same tightness in my stomach, the same bracing in my chest. But this time I let myself stay with it a bit longer instead of rushing past it. I didn't try to fix it or explain it away. I just let it be there and offered it a little kindness.

Something in me softened. Not because the numbers had changed, but because I had.

I'm heading into my chakra workshop this month with all of this still sitting with me, and I love how these old teachings and the newer science of the nervous system keep landing in the same place. When we feel safer, we feel freer.

So maybe the next time that familiar tightening shows up, whether it's about money or something else entirely, you'll pause for a moment, notice, and offer yourself that same small kindness.

With warmth and gratitude,
