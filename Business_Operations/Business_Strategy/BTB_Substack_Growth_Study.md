# Beyond the Bend Substack Growth Study

Updated May 1, 2026

## Plain English Summary

Substack is no longer only a newsletter tool. It is now a publishing, community, video, audio, social discovery, and paid subscription platform.

For Beyond the Bend, the strongest use of Substack is not to replace the website. It should become the public teaching and relationship home that sits beside the website.

The website should hold the stable business structure:

1. Studio classes
2. Livestream classes
3. Sanctuary membership
4. Courses
5. Contact
6. Affiliate recommendations

Substack should hold the ongoing living conversation:

1. Weekly essays
2. Short reflections
3. Practice invitations
4. Audio teachings
5. Occasional video or live sessions
6. Gentle paid depth work

The opportunity is real, but it should be built patiently. Substack income usually comes from trust, consistency, and a clear reason to pay. The best immediate move is to publish consistently, use the existing essay library, create a recognizable visual identity, and begin inviting readers into a deeper paid layer only after the free publication feels alive.

## What Is Happening On Substack Now

### 1. Substack Has Become A Creator Ecosystem

Substack now supports writing, audio, podcasts, video, live video, short social posts through Notes, recommendations, comments, chat, and paid subscriptions.

This matters because Beyond the Bend does not need to use Substack as only a newsletter archive. It can become a small public sanctuary where people read, listen, reflect, and slowly build a relationship with Laura.

### 2. Discovery Now Happens Inside Substack

Substack has its own discovery system. Notes, recommendations, restacks, comments, reader sharing, and the app can all send new readers toward a publication.

This means Laura should not only send emails. She should also participate lightly inside Substack itself.

The best version for Beyond the Bend:

1. Publish one full essay each week
2. Share two or three short Notes each week
3. Restack or comment on aligned writers
4. Invite readers into calm conversation
5. Track which posts bring subscribers

Source:

### 3. Paid Subscriptions Are Real, But They Are Not Instant

Substack is free to use until paid subscriptions are enabled. When paid subscriptions are active, Substack takes 10 percent of paid transactions, and Stripe fees also apply.

This is a good model for starting because there is no monthly platform cost for building the free publication.

The risk is emotional, not technical. Creators often expect income too quickly. The healthier approach is to build the free audience first, then introduce paid material as a natural deepening.

### 4. The Platform Rewards Relationship

Recent reporting suggests creators are moving to Substack because of community tools, not just because of email delivery. Recommendations, live video, cohosted streams, Notes, and reader interaction are part of the growth engine.

This is good news for Beyond the Bend because Laura already has a relational, reflective, teaching based voice. The brand does not need to become loud. It needs to become findable, steady, and intimate.

## What This Means For Beyond The Bend

Beyond the Bend is well positioned for Substack because it has:

1. A strong body of existing essays
2. A clear emotional and spiritual lane
3. A defined audience of women in midlife and beyond
4. A natural bridge to paid offers
5. A voice that feels personal, wise, and grounded
6. A growing library of teachings, practices, meditations, and classes

The strongest positioning is:

Reflections and practices for women learning to live, move, and age with more honesty, depth, and ease.

This should not be framed as generic yoga content. The more specific and human angle is better:

1. Midlife nervous system support
2. Embodied wisdom
3. Restorative yoga as a way of living

6. Slow practice for a fast, demanding world

## Recommended Substack Structure

### Free Layer

The free layer should build trust and habit.

Recommended free content:

1. Weekly essay
2. Short practice of the week
3. Personal reflection
4. Occasional studio note
5. Gentle invitation to classes, courses, or Sanctuary

The free layer should be generous. It should feel valuable on its own.

### Paid Layer

The paid layer should feel like deeper support, not withheld basics.

Recommended paid content:

1. Guided audio reflections
2. Nervous system practices
3. Deeper journal prompts
4. Monthly live practice or conversation
5. Behind the teaching reflections
6. Seasonal practice series
7. Occasional paid only essays with more personal depth

Suggested paid promise:

For paid subscribers, I offer a quieter room inside this work, with deeper practices, audio reflections, and monthly support for returning to yourself.

### Founding Member Layer

Substack allows founding member subscriptions for readers who want to pay more than the annual plan. This can be useful for people who already believe in Laura and want to support the work.

Recommended founding member framing:

For those who want to help this work grow and make it easier for more women to access.

## Recommended Posting Rhythm

### First Ninety Days

Use Substack mostly as a free publication while quietly preparing the paid layer.

Week by week rhythm:

1. One full essay each week
2. Two Notes each week
3. One image led reflection each week
4. One soft invitation every second week
5. One archive post from the existing library each week if there is no new essay

### After Ninety Days

Turn on paid subscriptions when the publication has rhythm, clarity, and a small group of engaged readers.

Possible paid rhythm:

1. Three free posts each month
2. One paid depth practice each month
3. One monthly paid live session or audio reflection
4. Occasional open thread for paid subscribers

## Image Strategy

Substack images should feel editorial, intimate, and trustworthy.

The goal is not to look like an ad. The goal is to help the reader feel the world of the piece before they read.

### Image Types To Use

1. Real photos of Laura  
Use when the piece is personal, embodied, relational, or teaching based.

2. Quiet nature images  
Use when the piece is seasonal, contemplative, spiritual, or metaphor based.

3. Studio or practice images  
Use when the piece connects to movement, nervous system regulation, classes, or Sanctuary.

4. Simple symbolic images  
Use when the essay has a clear metaphor, such as water, snow, gate, garden, fire, river, doorway, hands, cup, chair, mat, or window.

### Image Types To Avoid

1. Busy promotional graphics
2. Heavy text on images
3. Canva ad style layouts
4. Generic stock photos that feel too polished
5. Images that look like wellness marketing instead of a real person with a real practice

### Best Default Image Rule

If the essay is personal, choose Laura.

If the essay is poetic, choose nature.

If the essay teaches a practice, choose body, studio, mat, hands, or props.

If the essay is about transition, choose threshold, path, window, river, weather, or light.

## How Substack Should Connect To The Website

The website and Substack should have different jobs.

Website job:

1. Explain the business
2. Show current offers
3. Convert people into classes, courses, memberships, and contact
4. Hold evergreen pages

Substack job:

1. Build trust over time
2. Let Laura’s voice be encountered regularly
3. Grow an audience through email and Substack discovery
4. Create a paid relationship layer
5. Send warm readers back to the website

Every Substack post should have one calm pathway. Not five calls to action.

Examples:

1. If this reflection meets you, you may enjoy the Sanctuary.
2. If your body is asking for practice, you can find current classes here.
3. If you want the deeper version of this work, paid subscribers receive monthly practices and audio reflections.

## Income Path

The income path should be layered.

### Stage 1

Build free audience and trust.

Primary measures:

1. Total subscribers
2. Open rate
3. Comments
4. Notes engagement
5. Reader shares
6. Subscribers gained from each post

### Stage 2

Turn on paid subscriptions with one clear promise.

Suggested paid price:

1. Monthly: 8 dollars to 12 dollars
2. Annual: 80 dollars to 120 dollars
3. Founding member: 250 dollars or more

### Stage 3

Use paid Substack as the softer front door to deeper offers.

Paid Substack is not the whole business. It should support:

1. Sanctuary membership
2. Courses
3. Livestream practice
4. Retreats
5. Affiliate recommendations
6. One to one work
