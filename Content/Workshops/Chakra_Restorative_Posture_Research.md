# Chakra Restorative Posture Research

Created July 3, 2026

## Purpose

This note gathers a researched and practical posture map for The Sacred Journey with Zorana. It is meant to support the final workshop sequence, not replace Laura's teaching judgment.

The research supports three main principles:

1. Restorative yoga works best when the body is fully supported by props, the postures are held long enough for the nervous system to settle, and the student is not using muscular effort to stay in the shape.
2. Chakra posture choices are interpretive. The best choices for this workshop should match the energetic theme, the body area, the emotional tone, and the practical flow of the room.
3. Because this is a three hour Crown to Root journey with Reiki, the postures should feel safe, accessible, quiet, and spacious rather than performative.

## Recommendation For Crown

I would not make Child's Pose the second Crown posture unless Laura strongly wants it there. Child's Pose with forehead contact is beautiful, but it belongs more naturally to the Third Eye because of the brow and forehead contact, the inner gaze, and the quieting of visual attention.

For Crown, the strongest pair is:

1. Elevated Supported Savasana with legs over a bolster.
2. Supported Waterfall Pose.

This keeps the Crown spacious, receptive, and surrender based before the journey begins descending into more specific body centers.

Practical room note: because there will be fourteen people and not enough wall space, do not plan Crown around walls or chairs. Waterfall is the better second posture if it can be done from the same setup with minimal rearranging.

Suggested flow: begin with the legs resting over the bolster. For the second shape, students can bend the knees, place the feet down briefly, lift the pelvis just enough to slide the bolster under the back of the pelvis, and then let the legs float up toward the ceiling. Knees can stay softly bent. If this becomes effortful, students can simply return the legs to the bolster.

## Crown Breath Practice

Recommended breath practice: I Am, Safe and Held Breath.

This is the best fit for Crown because it is quiet, spacious, and meditative. It does not require hand positioning, breath retention, strong effort, or a particular breathing capacity. Students can remain in the supported shape and simply let the inhale carry "I am" and the exhale carry "safe and held."

Suggested cueing:

Let the breath be natural first. Nothing to deepen yet. As the body settles, silently hear "I am" with the inhale and "safe and held" with the exhale.

Why not Alternate Nostril Breath here: it is a beautiful practice for balancing the mind, but in this setting it asks people to lift an arm, manage the hand position, and think more than Crown needs. It may be better before practice, seated, or for Third Eye if you want a clearer concentration practice.

Why not a strong breath practice here: Crown should begin with surrender, spaciousness, and receptivity. Strong breathwork, long retentions, or complicated counting could pull people back into effort.

## Two Best Postures For Each Chakra

| Chakra | Posture One | Why It Fits | Posture Two | Why It Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crown, Sahasrara | Elevated Supported Savasana, head slightly lifted, legs over bolster | Best for surrender, spaciousness, and letting effort dissolve. The whole body is held, the breath can become natural, and students can feel supported by something larger than their own effort. | Supported Waterfall Pose | Gives the nervous system a clear signal of being held without needing a wall or chair. The lifted legs give a gentle sense of inversion while the pelvis remains supported. It keeps the crown and spine quiet while inviting a sense of openness and descent from the mind. |
| Third Eye, Ajna | Supported Child's Pose with forehead on bolster or floor | Best use of forehead contact. It draws attention inward, quiets the eyes, and gives the brow a physical resting point. This is the better home for the Child's Pose you were considering. | Supported Seated or Wide Leg Forward Fold with forehead supported | Also gives forehead pressure and inner gaze. It lets students feel clarity through stillness rather than through effort. |
| Throat, Vishuddha | Supported Fish with neck fully supported | Opens the throat, jaw, upper chest, and front body without strain. It gives space for humming breath or sound work while keeping the body receptive. | Supported Reclined Throat Opener over a low bolster or blanket roll | Softer than a big backbend. It lets the throat be exposed but not vulnerable, which fits the theme of truth returning in a safe space. |
| Heart, Anahata | Supported Heart Opener over bolster | The clearest restorative heart posture. It opens the ribs, chest, lungs, and arms while letting students feel held from behind. | Supported Reclined Bound Angle with arms and knees fully supported | Creates receiving without force. It connects heart, breath, and tenderness while softening the front body and pelvis. |
| Solar Plexus, Manipura | Supported Prone Belly Rest | Best for the diaphragm, belly, ribs, and center line. The gentle pressure into the belly can help students soften bracing without feeling exposed. | Supported Sphinx on bolster or low support | Gives a small, steady flame of lift without active striving. It supports personal power without making the posture forceful. |
| Sacral, Svadhisthana | Supported Reclined Butterfly | Best for pelvis, hips, lower belly, emotional permission, and flow. Full support under the thighs is essential so the hips do not grip. | Supported Side Lying Rest with bolster between knees | Very safe, contained, and fluid. It gives the pelvis and hips permission to soften without the vulnerability some people feel in open hip shapes. |
| Root, Muladhara | Rooted Constructive Rest, feet on floor, knees supported or strapped | Best for grounded safety. Students feel their feet, pelvis, and back body connected to the floor. This is excellent as the final root landing. | Supported Child's Pose over bolster, or Prone Earth Rest | Best if you want a second root posture that feels earthy, contained, and close to the ground. Use a bolster so it stays restorative and not effortful. |

## Suggested Crown Wording

Tonight we begin at the crown, not because there is anything to reach for, but because this is where so many of us live by the end of the day, in the mind, in the thinking, in the effort to hold everything together. The first invitation is not to fix that. It is simply to let the body be held enough that the mind no longer has to lead.

In this supported shape, allow the breath to breathe itself. Let the legs be heavy over the bolster. Let the head be held. Let the back of the body receive the floor. The crown asks, what supports me beyond my own effort?
