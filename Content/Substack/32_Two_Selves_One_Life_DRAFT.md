### Do You Feel Safe In Your Body?

*By Laura Harvey | Beyond the Bend Yoga*

I wonder if anyone has ever asked you that directly. Because when I finally sat with it honestly, really honestly, the answer surprised me.

I felt capable in my body. Practiced. Grateful for what it carries and what it does. But safe? That felt like a different question entirely.

Safety in the body is not about what the body can do. It is about whether the body itself feels like a place you can rest. Whether being in your own skin feels like something you move toward or something you quietly manage. Whether there is a quality of ease and belonging in the simple experience of being here, in the flesh, in your physical life.

And I think for a lot of us, if we are really honest, we have never quite felt that. Not because anything is wrong with us. But because the body holds its experience of safety in a completely different way than the mind does. It lives in the nervous system, in the tissue, in the places that formed their understanding of this world long before we had language or awareness or any capacity to make sense of what we were experiencing.

Peter Levine, the founder of Somatic Experiencing, spent his life studying exactly this. How the body holds its history. How it continues to run protective patterns long after the original need for them has passed. How slowly and gently and patiently it can begin to learn something different.

What he found, and what so many somatic practitioners have found since, is that the body cannot be talked into feeling safe. It cannot be convinced or inspired or understood into it. It learns safety through experience. Through slowness. Through the gradual accumulation of moments where it discovers that it is allowed to soften, that nothing terrible happens when it does, that it is welcome here in this life.

I find that both humbling and relieving. Humbling because it means there are no shortcuts. And relieving because it means there is nothing wrong with us for still being in the middle of it. The body simply has its own timeline. And it cannot be rushed into knowing what it is not yet ready to feel.

So much of what I think we carry, that persistent low level unease, the tension we cannot quite locate or release, the sense that something remains just out of reach no matter how much work we have done, lives right there. In a body that has not yet fully learned that it is safe to be here.

That is not a character flaw. It is simply the body doing what bodies do, holding what they have been given to hold, until something helps them learn that they can finally put it down.

So I want to ask you one more time, and I want you to feel into it rather than think about it.

Do you feel safe in your body?

If something in you hesitates, if there is a quiet no or a not really or a not sure living somewhere underneath, I want you to know that I am right there with you in that question. It is not a problem to fix today. It is simply something to notice. Something to get curious about. Because that hesitation is the body speaking. And it deserves to be heard.

## Practice of the Week

### Coming Into the Body

Find a comfortable position, either seated or lying down, and let your eyes close or soften your gaze toward the floor.

Take three slow breaths and let your body begin to settle.

Bring your awareness into your physical body, not your thoughts about your body but the actual sensations of it right now. The weight of you against the chair or the floor. The temperature of the air on your skin. The rise and fall of your breath in your chest and belly.

Just notice. Without trying to change anything or fix anything or make it feel different than it does.

Ask yourself gently, is there ease here? Is there tension? Is there a place that feels held or braced or slightly apart from the rest?

Whatever is here, let it be here. You are not trying to solve it. You are simply making contact with it and letting it know it has been noticed.

Stay with this for as long as feels right.

When you are ready, open your eyes and if you feel willing, pick up a pen and write from these questions.

When I dropped into my body just now, what did I find there?

Is there a quality of safety or ease present, and if so where do I feel it?

Is there a place that does not yet feel safe, and what does that feel like?

Write without editing. Just let whatever is true come through.

## A Closing Thought

Safety is not something we arrive at once and then have forever. It is something the body learns, slowly and in its own time, through experiences that tell it again and again that it is allowed to be here.

That is what I keep coming back to. Not the grand openings or the moments of profound clarity, beautiful as those are. The quiet, patient, ongoing work of helping the body feel that it belongs in this life. That it is welcome here. That it does not have to brace or hold itself apart from its own experience any longer.

If something stirred in you reading this today, if a question landed that you have not had words for until now, I would love to hear from you. I mean that genuinely.

We are just learning, together, what it means to finally feel at home.
