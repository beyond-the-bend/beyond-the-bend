# Jamie Postill Second Private Session Plan

Date: June 18, 2026

Length: Sixty minutes

Focus: Familiarity, gentle movement, physical support, and nervous system settling

## Intention

Jamie appeared to be having a difficult week when Laura saw her in class on Monday. This session should not ask her to go deeper simply because it is session two.

The intention is to offer enough familiarity that her body recognizes the space, while adding gentle movement that was missed during the first session.

The session can communicate:

```text
You do not have to be better today.

We can meet the body that arrived.

You have choice throughout the session.

Nothing needs to be forced or released.
```

## Before Beginning

Ask Jamie whether anything has changed since the last session, including medications, medical symptoms, sleep, iron treatment, pain, dizziness, or her care plan.

Because Jamie has experienced chest pain and heaviness, ask whether today's sensation is familiar or whether anything feels new, stronger, or medically concerning.

If she has new or worsening chest pain, significant difficulty breathing, faintness, unusual sweating, pain spreading into the arm, jaw, shoulder, or back, or another symptom that feels medically urgent, do not continue with the session. Encourage urgent medical assessment.

## How Much To Repeat

Repeat the parts that gave Jamie a felt sense of support.

Useful repetition:

1. A predictable explanation of the session
2. Sensory orienting
3. Choice around position, props, weight, silence, and touch
4. The acupressure holds that felt supportive
5. A slow and grounded ending

New elements:

1. A fuller somatic movement sequence
2. Supported twists
3. A slightly longer but still natural breath practice
4. A brief comparison between two supportive sensations rather than sustained attention on discomfort

## Session Flow

## 1. Arrival And Gentle Check In

Time: Eight minutes

Offer tea or water and let her settle into a supported seat.

Possible questions:

```text
How are you arriving today?

Would it feel better to tell me a little about the week, or begin with the body first?

Is there anything physically or medically different today that I should know?

What feels most in need of support right now?

Is there any part of last session you especially want to repeat?
```

Avoid inviting a detailed trauma narrative. Listen for her current capacity and shape the session around that.

Explain the plan:

```text
Today will feel similar to last time. We will begin with some gentle seated movement, move into a supported twist on each side, settle into a comfortable resting position, and then repeat some of the quiet acupressure holds that seemed supportive last time. We can change any part of that as we go.
```

## 2. Sensory Orienting

Time: Four minutes

Invite Jamie to notice the room without requiring her to close her eyes.

Possible guidance:

```text
Let your eyes move slowly around the room. There is no need to search for anything. Notice where your gaze naturally wants to pause.

Perhaps notice one colour, one shape, and one object that feels steady or pleasant enough to look at.

You might also notice the support beneath your feet and beneath your seat.
```

Ask:

```text
Would it feel better to keep your eyes open for the movement, or let them soften?
```

## 3. Gentle Somatic Movement

Time: Twelve minutes

Keep the movements small, slow, and optional. Encourage Jamie to remain well inside a comfortable range.

Suggested sequence:

1. Press the feet gently into the floor, then release.
2. Slowly shift weight toward one sitting bone and then the other.
3. Open and close the hands.
4. Circle the wrists slowly.
5. Lift the shoulders gently toward the ears, then let them settle.
6. Roll the shoulders backward and forward.
7. Turn the head slightly toward one side, return to centre, then turn toward the other side.
8. Gently tilt one ear toward one shoulder, return to centre, then change sides.
9. Seated pelvic rocking, allowing the spine to respond naturally.
10. Small seated spinal waves, moving between gentle rounding and returning upright.
11. Let the arms float forward or outward on an inhale and return on an exhale, only if that feels easy.
12. Finish by resting the hands somewhere that feels supportive.

Possible language:

```text
We are not trying to stretch anything open.

Let the movement be small enough that your body does not have to brace against it.

You can pause at any time.

Notice whether your body prefers movement or stillness in this moment.
```

Avoid full neck circles. Small turns and side bends place less demand on a tired or dizzy system.

## 4. Supported Twists

Time: Ten minutes

Offer one supported twist on each side, approximately four minutes per side with unhurried transitions.

Possible setup:

1. Place a bolster lengthwise beside her.
2. Let her sit with one hip near the end of the bolster.
3. Turn toward the bolster only as far as feels comfortable.
4. Support her torso, head, knees, and arms with additional blankets or pillows.
5. Let her choose the direction of her head.

Before settling:

```text
Does this feel supported, or does any part of you feel like it has to hold on?
```

While resting:

```text
Notice the places where the props are meeting you.

There is no need to deepen the twist.

Let the position come to you rather than asking your body to reach for it.
```

Come fully back to a neutral position between sides. Pause and ask whether she wants the second side.

Skip the twists if she is dizzy, nauseated, experiencing increased chest pressure, or simply does not want them.

## 5. Supported Rest And Guided Breath

Time: Eight minutes

Offer supported savasana or another resting position she preferred last time.

Possible supports:

1. Bolster beneath the knees
2. Blanket or pillow beneath the head
3. Light blanket for warmth
4. Weighted blanket across the thighs only if she asks for it and it does not affect breathing or increase chest discomfort

Begin by letting the breath remain completely natural.

Possible guidance:

```text
There is no correct breath to find.

Notice where breathing is easiest to feel today, perhaps at the nostrils, ribs, belly, or somewhere else.

If it feels comfortable, allow the exhale to finish without pushing it.

You might take three or four breaths where the exhale is just a little softer or longer than the inhale. Then let the breath return to its own rhythm.
```

If focusing on breath increases anxiety, return immediately to contact points, temperature, sound, or visual orientation.

## 6. Familiar Acupressure Holds

Time: Twelve minutes

It makes sense to repeat the holds that appeared helpful. Familiarity can support trust and predictability.

Ask for consent before beginning and again before moving to the chest, head, or wrists.

Possible sequence:

1. Occiput hold, approximately four minutes
2. Temple hold or gentle Yintang contact, approximately two minutes
3. Wrist hold, approximately three minutes on each side or divided according to her response

Optional chest related hold:

Only repeat contact at the sternum if Jamie clearly requests or consents to it today and there is no new or worsening chest symptom. A safer alternative is to invite Jamie to place her own hand on her upper chest while Laura offers contact at the wrist, shoulder, head, or another agreed area.

Announce every transition:

```text
I am going to remove my hands now.

If it still feels okay, I would like to move to your wrists next.

Would you like the same amount of pressure as last time, less pressure, or no touch here today?
```

Do not describe the holds as treating PTSD, regulating a specific nerve, or medically releasing the chest. Present them as quiet supportive touch that Jamie can accept, modify, or decline.

## 7. Integration And Closing

Time: Six minutes

Remove contact slowly and leave a short period of quiet. Because Jamie is not doing well this week, avoid a long unsupported silence.

Invite small movements and orient her back to the room.

Possible questions:

```text
What feels even slightly different now?

Was there a moment when your body felt more supported?

Was anything too much, unclear, or not useful today?

What would be realistic to carry into the next few days?
```

Offer one simple home resource rather than several.

Possible resource:

```text
Once a day, place both feet on the floor, look around the room, and slowly press the feet down for three breaths. Then release the effort and notice the support underneath you.
```

## If Jamie Arrives Very Depleted

Shorten the talking and movement rather than trying to complete the entire plan.

An alternative sixty minute emphasis could be:

```text
Ten minutes arrival and check in

Five minutes orienting

Eight minutes small seated movement

Ten minutes one supported twist or supported side lying

Fifteen minutes supported rest and familiar holds

Seven minutes quiet integration

Five minutes closing
```

One supported twist is enough. Repeating the acupressure is enough. The success of the session is not measured by how many practices Laura completes.

## Laura's Reminder

Jamie does not need novelty today. She needs steadiness, responsiveness, and permission to arrive exactly as she is.

The most important information from this session may be learning which familiar practices her body chooses again.

This is supportive somatic and restorative work. It does not replace Jamie's medical, psychiatric, or trauma therapy care.
